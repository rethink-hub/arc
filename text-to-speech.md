<div class="section-header">
  <span class="section-tag">Voice quality</span>
  <h2>The voice matters as much as the reading.</h2>
</div>

<p>A robotic voice is the reason many people try text-to-speech once and never again. For dyslexic and low-vision listeners especially, voice quality decides whether TTS becomes a daily tool or a demo you uninstall — so this is where AI Read puts deliberate effort.</p>

<h3>Automatic language detection and voice selection</h3>

<p>You never pick a language or a voice before reading. Arc detects the dominant language of the text on screen, then picks the best installed voice for that language automatically. Reading a Japanese article in an English interface? Arc finds the best Japanese voice installed. If nothing is installed for that language, it falls back to the closest voice in the same language family rather than refusing. The selection follows a strict order — highest-quality voice tiers first, then any installed voice for the exact language, then a family match — so you always get the best available sound.</p>

<h3>Natural voices are a free download — and Arc points you at them</h3>

<p>Here's the thing most TTS apps won't tell you: the difference between a robotic voice and a natural one is usually just which voice the OS has installed. Modern desktop systems ship excellent neural voices as free downloads — <strong>Premium/Enhanced voices on macOS, Natural/Neural voices on Windows</strong> — but most people never install them, so most apps quietly use the compact default and sound terrible.</p>

<p>Arc doesn't stay silent about it. If only the low-quality system voice is installed for your language, a <strong>Voice Quality</strong> card appears — in Settings and once as a nudge on the Arc menu — explaining that better voices are a free OS download, with a <strong>Get Voices</strong> button that opens the OS voice-download settings directly. Download a voice, and Arc picks it up immediately, no restart. The difference on your next read is the difference between a robot and a reader.</p>

<h3>Speed, pitch, and list-aware reading</h3>

<p>In <strong>Settings → Speech</strong> you get two sliders — <strong>Speed</strong> and <strong>Pitch</strong> — plus a <strong>Test Speech</strong> button that speaks a sample sentence at your current settings, so you can dial it in by ear. Values save immediately.</p>

<p>The other detail you'll notice within the first minute: AI Read reads lists like a person reads lists. Plain TTS engines read "1. Point one 2. Point two" as one run-on statement. Arc splits numbered and bulleted items into separate utterances with a real pause between points, while keeping prose sentences whole — never broken mid-clause. For study material and how-to content, that pause is the difference between following the steps and hearing a blur.</p>
