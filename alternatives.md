  <h2>The short version</h2>
  <p>There are two different kinds of AI assistant. A <strong>chatbot</strong> lives in its own app: you switch to it, paste your context, and switch back. A <strong>screen-aware assistant</strong> lives on top of your other apps: it reads what's already in front of you and acts without copy-paste. Both work; the right one depends on where you spend your day.</p>

<div class="content-section">
  <h2>1. Arc — the assistant that lives on your screen</h2>
  <p><strong>What it is:</strong> a floating AI assistant over any app. On Android it's a sidebar you swipe in from the screen edge; on macOS it's a Spotlight-style menu you summon with ⌃ Space. It reads the current screen, so "summarize this" needs no copy-paste.</p>
  <p><strong>Strengths:</strong> it acts on the actual screen content in Chrome, Gmail, or a PDF reader; Smart Extract turns events, contacts, and OTP codes into working buttons; custom prompts plus a 500+ community catalog, each bindable to a desktop shortcut; auto-off in 200+ banking and crypto apps.</p>
  <p><strong>Weaknesses:</strong> not on iPhone yet (Android, macOS and Windows are live); not a deep-Google assistant the way Gemini is; AI generation needs internet.</p>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> people who want AI help <em>inside</em> whatever they're already doing — reading, emailing, studying, texting. One Pro subscription covers Android, macOS and Windows. <a href="https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.rethink.arc" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Get Arc free →</a></p>
</div>

<div class="content-section">
  <h2>2. Google Gemini</h2>
  <p><strong>What it is:</strong> Google's flagship assistant and the Android default — a chat app with deep hooks into Gmail, Docs, Maps, and YouTube, plus image generation and live voice.</p>
  <p><strong>Strengths:</strong> unmatched Google integration; real screen-awareness on recent Pixel and Galaxy devices; a capable free tier with strong multimodal features.</p>
  <p><strong>Weaknesses:</strong> it's an app you switch to; screen-awareness is device-gated, so on a mid-range phone from another brand the headline features aren't there; no practical OTP extraction or call transcription.</p>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> Google-native users who want one assistant across Gmail, Docs, and their phone — especially with AI Pro.</p>
</div>

<div class="content-section">
  <h2>3. Circle to Search</h2>
  <p><strong>What it is:</strong> a built-in gesture on Pixel and Samsung Galaxy phones — long-press the home bar, circle anything on screen, and Google searches it.</p>
  <p><strong>Strengths:</strong> the fastest "what is this?" tool that exists, free and already on your phone, with AI overviews and translation folded in.</p>
  <p><strong>Weaknesses:</strong> it's a feature, not an assistant — no replies, saved summaries, structured extraction, or automation; Pixel and recent Samsung flagships only.</p>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> quick visual identification and homework-style lookups on a Pixel or Galaxy.</p>
</div>

<div class="content-section">
  <h2>4. Sider</h2>
  <p><strong>What it is:</strong> the app that popularized the "AI sidebar" — a panel beside any webpage with a choice of frontier models (GPT, Claude, and others).</p>
  <p><strong>Strengths:</strong> an excellent in-browser experience for research, summarization, and translation; model choice; a mature, polished product.</p>
  <p><strong>Weaknesses:</strong> native apps — your mail client, a PDF reader, a chat app — are invisible to it; Android is an afterthought; heavier paywall for daily use than the free tier suggests.</p>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> web research on desktop, especially if you want model choice inside a sidebar.</p>
</div>

<div class="content-section">
  <h2>5. Microsoft Copilot</h2>
  <p><strong>What it is:</strong> Microsoft's assistant, woven into Windows and Microsoft 365 — chat, image generation, and increasingly agentic features across Word, Excel, Outlook, and Teams.</p>
  <p><strong>Strengths:</strong> deep Windows and Office integration; free access to strong models; improving agentic features for Windows tasks.</p>
  <p><strong>Weaknesses:</strong> no cross-app screen awareness, especially on Android; the best features want the Microsoft ecosystem and often a 365 subscription.</p>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> Microsoft shops and Windows-first professionals.</p>
</div>

<div class="content-section">
  <h2>6. ChatGPT</h2>
  <p><strong>What it is:</strong> OpenAI's chatbot app — the default "talk to an AI" experience, with voice mode, image generation, file uploads, and Custom GPTs.</p>
  <p><strong>Strengths:</strong> the strongest general-purpose chatbot for reasoning and writing; the best spoken-conversation experience on mobile; works through uploaded PDFs, spreadsheets, and photos.</p>
  <p><strong>Weaknesses:</strong> you bring everything to it — copy, open, paste, switch back; it can't see your other apps or write back into the field you were typing in; the best models sit behind Plus.</p>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> anyone who wants the best standalone AI conversation and doesn't mind the copy-paste loop.</p>
</div>

<div class="content-section">
  <h2>7. Perplexity</h2>
  <p><strong>What it is:</strong> an "answer engine" — search that returns a synthesized, cited answer, with follow-ups that stay in context.</p>
  <p><strong>Strengths:</strong> every claim links to its source; fast research flows with Spaces and focused modes; a genuinely useful free tier.</p>
  <p><strong>Weaknesses:</strong> it answers about the world, not your screen; not a writing or automation tool; citation quality varies on niche topics.</p>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> research, fact-checking, and replacing the "google it" habit with something that reads the sources for you.</p>
</div>
