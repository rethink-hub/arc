  <h2>The short version</h2>
  <p>There are two fundamentally different kinds of AI assistant, and most "which app is best" arguments fall apart because the people arguing are talking about different kinds. A <strong>chatbot</strong> lives in its own app: you switch to it, type or paste your context, and switch back. A <strong>screen-aware assistant</strong> lives on top of your other apps: it reads what's already in front of you and acts without any copy-paste. Both approaches work. They solve different problems, and the right answer depends on where you spend your day.</p>

<div class="content-section">
  <h2>1. Arc — the assistant that lives on your screen</h2>
  <p><strong>What it is:</strong> a floating AI assistant that appears over any app you're using. On Android it's a sidebar you swipe in from the screen edge; on macOS it's a Spotlight-style menu you summon with ⌃ Space. In both cases it reads the current screen, so "summarize this" needs no copy-paste, and it runs the action without you leaving what you were doing.</p>

    <p style="font-size:0.9em; color:var(--text-muted);">Arc's menu summoned over Chrome on macOS — no app switch, no copy-paste. On Android, the same actions live in the edge-swipe sidebar.</p>

  <p><strong>Strengths:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>It works where you already are.</strong> Reading a long article in Chrome, an email in Gmail, a PDF in a reader — Arc acts on the actual screen content. No copying, no pasting, no switching apps.</li>
    <li><strong>It turns reading into doing.</strong> Smart Extract pulls events, contacts, meeting links, addresses, and OTP codes off the screen and gives each one a working button — add to calendar, save contact, copy the code before it expires.</li>
    <li><strong>It's yours to rewire.</strong> Write a custom prompt once ("explain this stack trace", "find the calories in this meal"), or add from a catalog of 500+ community actions. On desktop, any action can get its own global keyboard shortcut.</li>
    <li><strong>Privacy is designed in.</strong> It auto-disables in 200+ banking and crypto apps, never reads in the background, and asks consent before any screenshot.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><strong>Weaknesses:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>It's not on iPhone or Windows yet.</strong> Android and macOS are live; iOS and Windows are coming soon. If your whole life is in Safari on an iPhone, Arc can't help you today.</li>
    <li><strong>It's not a deep-Google assistant.</strong> It won't manage your calendar inbox the way Gemini's integrations do, and it doesn't try.</li>
    <li><strong>AI generation needs internet.</strong> It runs on cloud models, so this is not an offline assistant.</li>
  </ul>

  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> people who want AI help <em>inside</em> whatever they're already doing — reading, emailing, studying, texting — especially on Android and Mac.</p>
  <p><strong>Price:</strong> free to download with a weekly AI allowance; Pro removes the limits, one subscription across Android and macOS.</p>

<div class="content-section">
  <h2>2. Google Gemini</h2>
  <p><strong>What it is:</strong> Google's flagship AI assistant and the default on Android — a chat app with deep hooks into Gmail, Docs, Maps, YouTube, and the rest of the Google empire, plus image generation and live voice conversations.</p>
  <p><strong>Strengths:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Unmatched Google integration.</strong> If your life runs on Gmail, Calendar, and Docs, Gemini can reach into them in ways nothing else can.</li>
    <li><strong>Real screen-awareness on flagship hardware.</strong> On recent Pixel and Galaxy devices, Gemini can see and reason about what's on your screen — the gap with dedicated screen assistants is genuinely narrowing there.</li>
    <li><strong>A capable free tier</strong> and strong multimodal features — images, voice, video understanding.</li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Weaknesses:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Using it means leaving what you're doing.</strong> It's an app you switch to. On most non-Pixel devices there's no acting on your current screen mid-task.</li>
    <li><strong>No practical OTP extraction or call transcription</strong> — it won't grab a verification code before it expires.</li>
    <li><strong>Screen-awareness is device-gated.</strong> On a mid-range phone from another brand, the headline features quietly aren't there.</li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> Google-native users who want one assistant across Gmail, Docs, and their phone — especially if they pay for AI Pro.</p>
  <p><strong>Price:</strong> free tier; Google AI Pro subscription for the larger models and deeper integrations.</p>
</div>

<div class="content-section">
  <h2>3. Circle to Search</h2>
  <p><strong>What it is:</strong> a built-in Android gesture on Pixel and Samsung Galaxy phones — long-press the home bar, circle or tap anything on screen, and Google searches it. Homework problems, products, landmarks, landmarks in a photo, text in another language.</p>
  <p><strong>Strengths:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Frictionless visual lookups.</strong> The fastest "what is this?" tool that exists, and it's already on your phone.</li>
    <li><strong>Free, everywhere on supported devices</strong> — no account juggling, no subscription.</li>
    <li><strong>Improving constantly</strong> — AI overviews and translation have folded in nicely.</li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Weaknesses:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>It's a feature, not an assistant.</strong> It won't write replies, summarize a long document into saved notes, or automate anything.</li>
    <li><strong>Lookup-only means no memory and no follow-through.</strong> You can't save, chat, or extract structured data with one-tap actions.</li>
    <li><strong>Hardware-gated:</strong> Pixel and recent Samsung flagships only.</li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> quick visual identification and homework-style lookups on a Pixel or Galaxy.</p>
  <p><strong>Price:</strong> free.</p>
</div>

<div class="content-section">
  <h2>4. Sider</h2>
  <p><strong>What it is:</strong> the app that popularized the "AI sidebar" — a panel that sits beside any webpage in your browser with a choice of frontier models (GPT, Claude, and others) for summarizing, translating, and chatting about the page.</p>
  <p><strong>Strengths:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Excellent in-browser experience.</strong> Web research, page summarization, and translation in place, on desktop and iOS.</li>
    <li><strong>Model choice</strong> — you're not locked to one provider's model.</li>
    <li><strong>Mature, polished product</strong> with a large user base and steady development.</li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Weaknesses:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>It lives in the browser.</strong> Native apps — your mail client, a PDF reader, a chat app — are invisible to it.</li>
    <li><strong>Android is an afterthought</strong> compared to its desktop experience.</li>
    <li><strong>Heavier paywall</strong> for serious daily use than the free tier suggests.</li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> web research on desktop, especially if you want model choice inside a sidebar.</p>
  <p><strong>Price:</strong> freemium, with usage-based premium tiers.</p>
</div>

<div class="content-section">
  <h2>5. Microsoft Copilot</h2>
  <p><strong>What it is:</strong> Microsoft's AI assistant, woven into Windows and Microsoft 365 — chat, image generation, and increasingly agentic features across Word, Excel, Outlook, and Teams.</p>
  <p><strong>Strengths:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Deep Windows and Office integration.</strong> If you work in Microsoft 365 all day, the 365-tier Copilot features are genuinely useful.</li>
    <li><strong>Free access to strong models</strong> for everyday chat and image generation.</li>
    <li><strong>Improving agentic features</strong> for automating Windows tasks.</li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Weaknesses:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>No cross-app screen awareness.</strong> On Android especially, it's a competent chatbot that can't overlay or act on other apps.</li>
    <li><strong>The best features want the Microsoft ecosystem</strong> — and often a 365 subscription on top.</li>
    <li><strong>Android experience is an afterthought</strong> next to the Windows one.</li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> Microsoft shops and Windows-first professionals.</p>
  <p><strong>Price:</strong> free tier; advanced features with Microsoft 365 subscriptions.</p>
</div>

<div class="content-section">
  <h2>6. ChatGPT</h2>
  <p><strong>What it is:</strong> OpenAI's chatbot app — the default "talk to an AI" experience for hundreds of millions of people, with voice mode, image generation, file uploads, and Custom GPTs.</p>
  <p><strong>Strengths:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>The strongest general-purpose chatbot.</strong> Reasoning, writing quality, and breadth are the benchmark the others are measured against.</li>
    <li><strong>Excellent voice mode</strong> — genuinely the best spoken-conversation experience on mobile.</li>
    <li><strong>A file-and-image powerhouse:</strong> upload a PDF, a spreadsheet, or a photo and it'll work through it.</li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Weaknesses:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>You have to bring everything to it.</strong> Copy the text, open the app, paste, switch back. The friction is the whole cost for in-app tasks.</li>
    <li><strong>It can't see your other apps or act on your screen.</strong> No overlays, no OTP extraction, no write-back into the field you were typing in.</li>
    <li><strong>The best models sit behind Plus.</strong></li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> anyone who wants the best standalone AI conversation and doesn't mind the copy-paste loop.</p>
  <p><strong>Price:</strong> free tier; Plus subscription for the strongest models.</p>
</div>

<div class="content-section">
  <h2>7. Perplexity</h2>
  <p><strong>What it is:</strong> an "answer engine" — search that returns a synthesized, cited answer instead of ten blue links, with follow-up questions that stay in context.</p>
  <p><strong>Strengths:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Cited, current answers.</strong> Every claim links to its source, which makes it the best tool here for research you might repeat to someone else.</li>
    <li><strong>Fast, focused research flows</strong> — follow-up questions, Spaces for organizing threads, focused search modes.</li>
    <li><strong>The free tier is genuinely useful</strong> for everyday questions.</li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Weaknesses:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>It answers about the world, not your screen.</strong> Nothing on this page — no reading your apps, no acting on what's in front of you.</li>
    <li><strong>Not a writing or automation tool.</strong> Drafting a reply in your own voice isn't what it's built for.</li>
    <li><strong>Citation quality varies</strong> on niche topics — like any search-grounded AI, it can over-trust a weak source.</li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> research, fact-checking, and replacing the "google it" habit with something that reads the sources for you.</p>
  <p><strong>Price:</strong> free tier; Pro subscription for more and stronger searches.</p>
</div>

  <details>
    <summary>What is the best AI assistant app in 2026?</summary>
    <p>It depends on where you need it. For general chat, ChatGPT and Gemini lead. For research with citations, Perplexity. For an assistant that works inside every app on Android and macOS — summarizing, extracting, and replying on the screen you're already on — that's Arc's category, and it's the only tool here built for it.</p>
  </details>
  <details>
    <summary>What makes a screen-aware AI assistant different from a chatbot?</summary>
    <p>A chatbot lives in its own app: you copy or retype your context to bring it there. A screen-aware assistant reads the app you're already in and acts on it — no copy-paste, no switching. The trade-off is that screen assistants need overlay permissions and careful privacy design, which is why Arc reads only on demand and stays blind in banking apps.</p>
  </details>
  <details>
    <summary>Is Arc a good alternative to Google Gemini?</summary>
    <p>For different jobs. Gemini is stronger as a general assistant with deep Google integrations. Arc is stronger at acting on whatever screen is in front of you — summaries, OTP extraction, flashcards, read-aloud, custom actions — without leaving the app. Many people run both.</p>
  </details>
  <details>
    <summary>Do any of these assistants work inside other apps?</summary>
    <p>Only the screen-aware ones. Arc overlays any app on Android and macOS. Circle to Search overlays for lookups only. Sider is limited to the browser. Gemini, Copilot, ChatGPT, and Perplexity are apps you switch into, not overlays.</p>
  </details>
  <details>
    <summary>Which AI assistant is free?</summary>
    <p>Circle to Search is entirely free. Arc, Gemini, Copilot, ChatGPT, Sider, and Perplexity all have usable free tiers, with subscriptions for higher limits or stronger models. Arc's free plan includes a weekly AI allowance; Pro is one subscription across Android and macOS.</p>
  </details>
  <details>
    <summary>Can one assistant do everything?</summary>
    <p>Not yet — and be suspicious of any that claim to. General chatbots have the best models but don't act on your screen; screen-aware assistants act everywhere but aren't chat-first products. The practical setup in 2026 is one of each.</p>
