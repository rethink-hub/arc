## What makes a great AI summary app Android users keep

Before the list, the criteria I used. An AI summary app for Android should:

- **Work on text you didn't copy.** The whole copy-paste-into-a-chatbot loop breaks when you're reading in Gmail, Chrome, or a PDF viewer. The best apps read the screen directly.
- **Offer summary styles.** Bullet points, paragraph, key takeaways, ELI5 — different texts need different shapes.
- **Handle long documents** without truncating mid-argument.
- **Let you ask follow-up questions** about the text instead of regenerating a new summary each time.
- **Summarize more than articles** — emails, threads, screenshots, videos, images with text in them.

Every app below does some of this. Arc does all of it, which is why it's #1 — and I'll show the receipts.

## 2. Summary — AI Note Taker (best for meetings)

If your summary needs are 90% meetings, [Summary - AI Note Taker](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.labhouse.audioapp) is the strongest pure play. Record a meeting, get a transcript, get a structured summary with action items. 4.6 stars from 125K+ ratings says it's doing that one thing well.

**Where it falls short for general use:** it summarizes conversations you record, not text on your screen. You can't point it at a Gmail thread or a web article. It's a meeting tool with a summary feature, not a screen summarizer.

**Best for:** back-to-back meeting days.

## 3. Read AI (best for call recaps and scheduling)

Read AI records calls, transcribes, summarizes, and tracks who talked most. The mobile app is solid if your summary workflow is "everything that happens in a meeting."

**Trade-off:** like Summary AI, it's recording-based. No on-screen text summarization. Also pricier once you're past the free tier.

**Best for:** teams that live in calls.

## 4. AI Summarizer — Summary AI (best simple free pick)

The straightforward pick. Paste text, choose length, get a summary. It's fast, it's free, it's honest about what it is.

**Trade-off:** copy-paste only — if the text is on your screen, you do the copy-paste work yourself. Some users report ads on the free tier.

**Best for:** occasional paste-and-summarize jobs.

## 5. QuillBot (best for students at a desk)

QuillBot's summarizer is excellent — key-sentences mode vs paragraph mode, adjustable length. It's web-based, so "app" means a Chrome tab. On a phone it works, but it's a browser tool, not an app that sees your screen.

**Best for:** essays and papers when you're already at a keyboard.

## 6. Notion AI (best if you already live in Notion)

Notion AI summarizes pages inside your workspace. If your reading and notes already happen in Notion, it's convenient. As a general-purpose pick it's constrained to Notion content — you can't summarize Gmail or a PDF in another app.

**Best for:** Notion users summarizing their own docs.

## 7. Perplexity (best for summarizing web answers)

Perplexity summarizes the web with citations when you ask a question. It's a research tool with summarization at its core, not a screen summarizer — you're inside Perplexity's app the whole time.

**Best for:** question-style research, not summarizing what's in front of you.

For summarizing text that's on your screen across any app, Arc is the strongest pick — it reads what's visible without copy-paste and offers multiple summary formats. If your summaries are mostly of meetings you attend, a recording-based tool like Summary - AI Note Taker fits better.

**Is there a free AI summary app Android users can trust?**

Yes. Arc's summary feature works on the free tier, and AI Summarizer — Summary AI is free paste-only. Watch the free tiers of recording-based apps — most cap monthly minutes.

**How does an AI summary app Android users love actually read the screen?**

It uses the Android accessibility service to read the text currently displayed by any app, sends it to an AI model, and shows the result in a floating panel. Arc does this via a floating sidebar you tap once — you never leave the app you're reading in.

**Can an AI summary app Android tool handle images or screenshots?**

Arc can — it processes what's visually on screen, not just selectable text. So a screenshot of a long chat, a slide from a video, or a photo of a document can be summarized. Most paste-based tools can't.

**Do these apps work offline?**

No serious AI summary app for Android works fully offline — the summarization runs on cloud models. Arc processes requests server-side and doesn't store your screen text beyond your saved summaries.
