Full transparency: I build [Arc](/), an AI screen assistant for Android. It's on this list because people use it and it wins at what it does — but I've tested each app below against the same standard: *does it actually save time every day?* Where a competitor beats Arc, I say so.

Here are the nine AI apps that earned a spot on my phone in 2026.

## How I Tested

Every app below went on my main phone — not a test device — for at least two weeks, doing the work I would have done anyway: reading long articles on a commute, answering messages in five apps, pulling details out of screenshots, sitting through meetings. Three questions decided whether an app stayed:

1. **Did I reach for it unprompted?** An app I only opened because I was reviewing it does not belong on a "best" list.
2. **How many taps from "I want this" to "I have this"?** The gap between a good model and a useful app is almost always this number.
3. **What happened the first time it was wrong?** Anything that failed confidently and quietly got marked down hard.

Nothing here is sponsored, and no one paid for placement. Prices are what I saw in September 2026; check before you subscribe.

## 1. Arc — AI Screen Assistant (Best overall assistant)

**Free · Android + macOS**

The category Arc created: a floating AI sidebar that works on top of every app. Swipe from the screen edge and it appears over whatever you're doing — no switching, no copy-pasting.

**What it does:**
- Summarizes articles, PDFs, emails — with read-aloud for commutes
- Drafts replies and rewrites text using your saved personal info
- Extracts events, addresses, and OTP codes from any screen (tappable, saved before they expire)
- Transcribes and summarizes phone calls
- Custom AI actions for your repetitive routines

**Who it's for:** anyone who wants AI help without leaving their current app. Especially strong if you read a lot on your phone or handle lots of messages.

**Honest limit:** it's a screen assistant, not a full chatbot platform — for deep research sessions you might still open a dedicated chatbot.

## 2. Google Gemini (Best built-in assistant)

**Free / AI Premium · Android + iOS**

Gemini replaced Google Assistant on modern Android and it is now genuinely good. The screen-awareness on Pixel and Galaxy — asking about what's in front of you without describing it first — is the feature that changed my mind about it. With AI Premium it also lands inside Gmail and Docs, which is where it stops being an assistant app and starts being infrastructure.

**What it does well:** anything touching Google's own surfaces. Pulling a flight out of Gmail, drafting in Docs, answering from your calendar. No third-party app has that access and none will.

**Who it's for:** people whose life is already in Google's apps. If your email, documents and calendar are all Google, the integration is worth more than any model difference.

**Honest limit:** you leave your current app to use it. That sounds trivial until you count it — the context switch costs a few seconds and, more importantly, breaks whatever you were doing. Gemini is also uneven across third-party apps in a way it is not across Google's own.

## 3. Circle to Search (Best built-in feature)

**Free, built in · Pixel and Samsung flagships**

Long-press the navigation bar, circle anything on screen. It is the fastest "what is this?" tool on any phone, full stop — products in a photo, a landmark in a video, a homework problem. The interaction is so direct that people who never open an AI app use this one daily without thinking of it as AI.

**What it does well:** identification and lookup, in under two seconds, from anywhere.

**Who it's for:** everyone with a supported phone. There is no reason not to use it.

**Honest limit:** lookups only. It will tell you what something is; it will not summarize the article, draft the reply, or do anything with the answer. It also ships only on Pixel and Samsung flagships, so a mid-range phone doesn't get it.

## 4. ChatGPT (Best general chatbot)

**Free / $20 Plus · Android, iOS**

Still the deepest general-purpose chatbot, and the Android app has stopped feeling like a port. Voice mode is the standout — genuinely conversational, good enough to use while walking. Custom GPTs give it range nothing else on this list matches.

**What it does well:** long, winding problems. Anything where you need to think out loud for twenty minutes and have the thread hold together.

**Who it's for:** anyone doing real work with AI rather than quick lookups. If you only install one chatbot, this is still the one.

**Honest limit:** the same as Gemini's, minus the OS integration. It lives in its own app, isolated from whatever you were doing. Every use starts with a copy and ends with a paste.

## 5. Sider (Best browser sidebar)

**Free / from $10/mo · iOS, Desktop, Web**

The original "AI sidebar": a panel beside any webpage, with frontier-class models behind it. On desktop it is excellent — page summaries, follow-up questions, and comparisons across tabs without losing your place.

**What it does well:** sustained web research. Reading six sources on one question and keeping the assistant alongside all of them.

**Who it's for:** desktop researchers. Writers, analysts, anyone whose work is mostly tabs.

**Honest limit on Android:** the mobile experience is a real step down from the desktop one. Sider's strength is the browser sidebar, and Android's browsers don't give it the same room. If you found Sider through desktop and expected parity on your phone, you will be disappointed.

## 6. Otter.ai (Best meeting transcription)

**Free / from $8.33/mo · Android, iOS, Web**

If you sit in meetings, Otter records, transcribes, and summarizes them, with speaker labels that are mostly right. The summaries have got noticeably better — they now pull action items rather than just compressing the transcript.

**What it does well:** scheduled, multi-person meetings. It joins calendar calls on its own, which removes the failure mode where you remember to record after the important part.

**Who it's for:** anyone in more than about five meetings a week. Below that the free tier covers you; above it the subscription pays for itself the first time you skip taking notes.

**Honest limit:** meetings, not phone calls. Arc transcribes *phone* calls; Otter covers *scheduled* ones. They overlap less than the descriptions suggest, and if you need both you will run both.

## 7. Perplexity (Best for research)

**Free / $20/mo · Android, iOS**

Cited, current answers in a clean mobile app. The distinction that matters: Perplexity answers with sources attached rather than asking you to trust it, which makes it the right tool whenever being wrong has a cost. The Comet browser push is making it more agentic every month.

**What it does well:** questions about the present. Prices, specs, what happened last week, whether a claim is actually true.

**Who it's for:** people who check things. If your instinct on receiving an answer is to want the source, this is built for you.

**Honest limit:** it is a research tool, not a writing tool. Asking it to rewrite your email is using a microscope as a hammer — it works, but you bought the wrong instrument.

## 8. Notion AI (Best for note-takers)

**$10/mo add-on · Android, iOS, Web**

If your second brain lives in Notion, its AI summarizes, searches, and drafts inside your actual workspace — against your own pages rather than a generic index. Q&A across a messy workspace is the feature that justifies it.

**What it does well:** knowing your context without being told. It has already read everything you wrote.

**Who it's for:** committed Notion users, and only them.

**Honest limit:** the pricing is per-member, which stings for solo users and gets expensive for teams. And it is worth nothing if your notes are somewhere else — this is an add-on to a commitment you have already made, not a reason to make it.

## 9. Tasker + AI plugins (Best for automation nerds)

**~$3 one-off · Android**

Not an AI app itself. Tasker is the automation engine Android power users have run for over a decade; pair it with an AI API and you can build things no product on this list ships — reacting to a notification's content, chaining across apps, running on a schedule or a location.

**What it does well:** the automation you specifically need and no one else asked for.

**Who it's for:** people who enjoy the building. That is not a hedge — the enjoyment is the prerequisite.

**Honest limit:** you are the developer, the tester, and the support desk. Where Arc's custom actions are a prompt in a text box, this is an afternoon. The payoff is that the ceiling is much higher.

## What Didn't Make It

A few apps I tested and left off, since the omissions are usually the question:

- **Copilot** on Android is capable, but nothing it does is better than what Gemini already does natively on the same phone.
- **Claude's** mobile app is strong at long-form writing and reasoning, and I use it on desktop — but on a phone it competes directly with ChatGPT and doesn't yet win on the mobile experience.
- **The various "AI keyboard" apps** all ask you to replace your keyboard, which is a large permission for a small gain. Screen-level tools get the same job done without the swap.
